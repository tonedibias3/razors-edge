    How to read this: a leg "covers" when the team beats its line after the teaser points are added. For each leg we look at every game since 2015 where a team had the same line, and count how often it would have covered the teased number. "Needs" is the share of legs that must win, on average, for your odds to break even. Spreads here come from one free data source (probably the closing line), so type your book's number over it. Past results do not predict a single game; this is a way to spot weak legs and compare the 6, 6.5 and 7 point options.

    Your lineup

    Opp rank shows how the defense has done against that position over its last 8 games, the way ESPN and DraftKings show it: 32nd (green) allowed the most points, an easy matchup, and 1st (red) allowed the fewest. The number under it is the points per game above or below the league average. How the projection works: it starts from each player's recent DraftKings scoring (weighted toward his latest games), then adjusts for how he is used, how the opponent has defended his position, and how many points his team is expected to score. Moves up from a good matchup are shrunk by half because they did not hold up in testing, while cuts for players running hot were reliable. Floor and ceiling are the range real scores landed in 60% of the time in the test. Typical miss is about 5 points per player, so treat the numbers as a ranking aid. “Basic” means a short history or low usage, so the number is just a discounted recent average, and defenses use a simple rule based on the opponent’s expected score. Injuries come only from the Status column in your file: players listed OUT, IR or doubtful get no projection. You can type your own projection into any box, for example when a backup is starting.
